In Defense of the Slow Web

Feeds optimize for the new. Gardens optimize for the true. A case for publishing like a gardener instead of a broadcaster.

The web was supposed to be a library. Somewhere along the way it became a river — everything flowing past, nothing staying long enough to be read twice. The river model rewards the new over the true. It rewards the loud over the careful. And it rewards us for consuming, never for returning.

Against the river

A feed is a machine for converting attention into novelty. Its unit of time is the refresh. Every design decision follows: timestamps in the corner, infinite scroll, the quiet pressure that says there is always more, and it is always newer.

The garden is a different machine. Its unit of time is the season. Things are planted, tended, revised, and left to grow. A garden does not shout. It waits — and waiting is not the absence of value, it is the condition of it.

Publish, then revise

The broadcast model treats publication as an event: a thing is finished, released, and frozen. The garden model treats it as a stage: an idea is shared when it is alive enough to be useful, and revised as it grows. The same essay can be a seedling in spring and an evergreen in autumn.

This is not laziness dressed as philosophy. Revision is the core of writing. The garden simply makes the revision visible — a public record of thinking that changes its mind, which is the most honest thing a thinker can publish.

Slow is a feature

Attention is the only scarce resource left, and the slow web respects it. A page that asks for ten quiet minutes instead of ten reflexive seconds is not inefficient. It is a different kind of institution — closer to a reading room than a billboard.

A garden is not a faster way to broadcast. It is a slower way to mean something.

I keep this site as a small protest against the river. There is no infinite scroll here, no algorithm deciding what I should read next. There is only a patch of ground, a few careful essays, and the patience to let them grow. If you are reading this slowly, the protest is already working.
